Aims of the course:
The course is designed to show and master the principles of the econometric model building with emphasis on results' economic interpretation and verification.
The course presupposes knowledge of statistics, econometrics and economics at bachelor level.
 
Learning outcomes and competences:
Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to build a simple economic model, find appropriate data and use standard econometric tools to find an answer to research question.
 
Course contents:
1) Repeating basic concepts, Gauss Markov assumptions, structure of empirical research
2) Heteroskedasticity and robust error estimation
3) Introduction to time series.
4) Deterministic and stochastic trend, random walk, white noise.
5) Autoregressive models, moving average models.
6) ARMA, ARIMA, ADL and their derivatives.
7) Stationarity, unit root testing.
8) Instrumental variables and simultaneous equations.
9) Panel data: introduction and panel structure.
10) Panel data: panel data models.
11) Logistic regression (logit) and probit.
12) Cointegration, error correction models (ECM) and their applications.
13) Vector autoregression (VAR) and its applications.

Special requirements and details:
Submission of homework is a prerequisite for successful completion of the course. In the case of a grade of 4+, the student may retake the Final test. This course has been supported by the European Union, European Structural and Investment Funds, Operational Programme Research, Development and Education. To pass the course, the student must meet two conditions: (1) obtain at least 60 points overall, (2) obtain at least 50% of the points on the final test. Students scoring 50‑59 on the final test and students scoring more than 60, less than 50% on the final test, but more than 40% on the final test will be eligible for an additional attempt at the final test during an alternate date.
